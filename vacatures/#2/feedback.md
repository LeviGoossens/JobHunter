# Recruiter feedback: motivation letter

**Letter score: 7.5/10**

The letter is thoughtful, relevant and honest. It presents a promising junior candidate with a strong match to the technology stack, but there is a significant gap with the advertised medior experience.

**Recruiter decision:** If TSG is open to hiring and mentoring a junior, an introductory conversation would be worth considering. If the team needs someone who can immediately operate independently at medior level, more experienced applicants would likely be prioritized. The vacancy values technical independence and responsibility, not just years of experience, so applying is reasonable.

## Strengths

- **Strong technical relevance:** C#, .NET, Blazor, Entity Framework and API experience directly match the vacancy.
- **Concrete examples:** The multi-tenant migration, data-import tool and user interviews show more than a list of technologies.
- **Relevant motivation:** Interest in maintaining applications over time fits TSG's mix of new development and modernization.
- **Honesty:** The letter acknowledges the experience gap rather than presenting a junior as a medior developer.
- **Professional tone:** The Dutch is clear, natural and appropriate.

## Improvements

1. **Show the result of one contribution.** Explain, if you can substantiate it, what the import tool made easier or what the migration enabled.
2. **Address testing if you have relevant experience.** TSG explicitly asks for unit-testing experience. If you have written tests, briefly describe what you tested and which tools you used. Don't claim experience you don't have.
3. **Make the junior paragraph more confident.** Acknowledge the gap without making it the central point. For example:

   > Ik ben een junior developer en beschik nog niet over enkele jaren professionele .NET-ervaring. Wel breng ik een relevante technische basis mee en heb ik ervaring met het ontwikkelen en verbeteren van applicaties voor echte gebruikers.

4. **Tighten repetition.** Maintenance, quality, documentation, feedback and learning appear more than once. Combining these points would make room for a concrete outcome or testing example.
5. **Clarify the code-review claim if needed.** The letter says you now maintain Webshop-PXL independently but also mentions code reviews. A recruiter might wonder who reviews the code. Distinguish between reviews during the group project and your current independent work, if that reflects what happened.

## Bottom line

This is a credible letter worth sending. Its main limitation is the experience mismatch, not the writing. One concrete result and, if applicable, evidence of testing would give a recruiter a stronger reason to look beyond that gap.
